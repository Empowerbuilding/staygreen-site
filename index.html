The Problem

You Hire the Contractor.
You Keep the Risk.

The majors have ISN and Avetta gating every contractor at the door. Independents run on relationships, text messages, and a file cabinet. The liability doesn't care about your size.

The Incident Report Names You

A contractor's hand gets hurt on your lease. First questions: was he H2S-current? Did he have your site orientation? Who signed him onto the job? Without records, you're the one answering those questions.

The Auditor Rolls Up

OSHA and your insurer will ask for the JSAs and the training records. A paper tailgate in a truck three miles away isn't documentation — it's a story.

"Trust Me, He's Got His Cards"

Every contractor says their people are trained. Nobody's checked the dates. An expired DISA or a missing card is exactly the gap that surfaces at the worst moment.

The Certificate Nobody Read

Your contractor's COI expired two months ago, or the additional-insured endorsement isn't there. The claim gets denied — on your lease, with your name on the PO.

The Gate Turnaway

A crew shows up, credentials come up short, the whole truck goes home. Your downtime, your schedule, your angry customer — and the job waits on paperwork nobody can find.

The Two Levels

Two Levels of Risk. Both Handled.

Every company you let on your lease — primes and their subs — and every hand they bring.

The Company Level

Signed MSA on file. COIs read word-by-word — additional insured, waiver of subrogation, the wording that decides claims. W-9 and EMR tracked. An unsigned or uninsured contractor never reaches your work order.

The Worker Level

Every hand checked against your rules: H2S Awareness — the card is current, not promised. DISA — drug screen in date. Plus every other card you require: your rules, every hand, every job.

A hand short a card never reaches the manifest. You see it before they see the gate.
